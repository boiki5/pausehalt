2. What the app stores, and why we cannot see it

PauseHalt runs entirely in your browser. When you save a log entry, the app writes it to localStorage — a small storage area belonging to your browser on your device. Entries can include what you write in these fields:

  • What happened
  • What you felt
  • What old memory it brought up
  • The emotion you selected and the response you chose
  • The date and time

This information is never transmitted anywhere. It is not sent to us, to a server, or to any third party. There is no network request involved in saving it. We have no database of user entries, because there is no database at all. Practically:

  • We cannot read your entries. Not on request, not by subpoena, not by accident.
  • We cannot recover them for you. If you clear your browser data, use private mode, switch browser or device, or your device is wiped, the entries are gone permanently. There is no backup.
  • We cannot delete them for you. You do that yourself — entry by entry in My Week, or by clearing your browser's site data for the app.

Because the entries sit on your device, anyone who can unlock it and open the app can read them. This matters if you share a phone or laptop, or if your safety depends on a family member not seeing what you write. Use a screen lock, and consider what you commit to writing.

If you use "Copy my week as text", the content goes to your device's clipboard and then wherever you paste it. Once pasted into an email, note or message, this policy no longer covers that copy — it is governed by the service you pasted it into.
